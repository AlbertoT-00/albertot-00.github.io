About

I am a PhD student at the Department of Engineering and Architecture of the University of Trieste, supervised by Prof. Erica Salvato. I work on coordination in multi-agent systems learned through reinforcement learning, with attention to safety, and on decentralized formation control of drone swarms.

I studied Industrial Engineering and then Electrical Energy and Control Systems Engineering in Trieste. Control theory was what caught my interest first. From controllers designed on a model I moved to controllers learned from data, then to reinforcement learning, and finally to problems where several agents have to learn to act together.

Research interests

How can a group of agents, each acting on local information, learn to coordinate reliably and safely?

My path into the topic:

  1. Control theory designing controllers from a model of the system
  2. Learning-based control building controllers from data instead of from a model alone
  3. Reinforcement learning learning a control policy through interaction with the system
  4. Coordination in multi-agent systems several learning agents that must act together on a shared environment

What I work on

Coordination in multi-agent systems

This is the core of my research. A team of agents can only succeed if each one adapts to what the others are doing, while all of them are learning at the same time: every agent is solving a problem that the others keep changing. I am interested in how coordinated behaviour emerges from this process, and in methods based on centralized training and decentralized execution, such as MADDPG, where agents share information while learning but must act on their own once deployed.

Variance as a measure of safety

An average reward says little about how a system behaves in its worst moments. Two policies with the same mean performance can be very different in practice if one of them occasionally produces collisions or erratic manoeuvres. I use the variance of the agents' behaviour and outcomes, not only their mean, to assess how safe and predictable a learned policy is, and to steer learning towards policies that are reliable rather than just good on average.

Credit assignment and reward design

When a team succeeds or fails, it is hard to tell which agent was responsible. I look at how the structure of the reward, from purely shared to individual and mixed formulations, shapes what each agent learns, and why some designs lead to coordination while others lead to agents getting in each other's way.

Coordination under partial observability

Real agents only see their neighbourhood, so they have to coordinate with an incomplete picture of the team. I study how the observation range affects coordination: seeing too little prevents it, while seeing more is not always better, since it also makes the learning problem larger and harder.

Coordination on real vehicles

Formation control of drone swarms is where coordination meets physics. I work on decentralized controllers that keep a group of drones in formation using only local information, and I care about taking them out of simulation and onto real vehicles, where noise, delays and model errors show what actually works.

Projects

Reward and perception in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning

With E. Medvet and E. Salvato

Cooperative agents trained with MADDPG in continuous control under partial observability. I study how the observation radius and the structure of the reward change coordination, and what this says about credit assignment among agents.

Decentralized formation control with impedance matching

Drone Laboratory, with F. Blanchini, G. Fenu, F. A. Pellegrino and E. Salvato

Formation control algorithms for multi-drone systems, developed in Python, tested in Gazebo and then validated on real drones flying in the laboratory arena.
